# rawr-gecko-grip — Original Narration

This gecko can sprint up glass without glue, suction, or sticky liquid. The secret begins under each wide toe, where ridges divide into dense microscopic hairs. Each hair branches again, creating enormous contact with the surface at tiny scales. At that distance, weak molecular attractions add up across the whole foot. To let go, the gecko changes the angle of its toes and peels them away. So the grip is powerful, reversible, and dry: geometry turns tiny forces into a climbing system.
